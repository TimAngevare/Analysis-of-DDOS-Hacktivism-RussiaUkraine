What one row is. A row in the source data is one news story, so stories about the same target within a week are clustered into one attack event. 7,076 events, of which 2,566 name an actor; the rest have a target but nobody attributed to it. Counting events rather than articles stops a heavily covered attack outweighing a hundred quiet ones.

Side is hand-coded, not extracted. Each named collective is coded Russia-aligned, Ukraine-aligned, state-linked, criminal or unaligned in dashboard/actors.py, from public reporting and the groups' own channels, with the reason recorded next to each entry. Spelling variants are merged (NoName, NoName057, DDoSia all become NoName057(16)), and mentions of techniques, nationalities and news outlets that the extractor labelled as groups (DDoS, pro-Russian hackers, GBHackers) are dropped rather than drawn as actors. Dropping bare pro-Russian hackers means the Russia-aligned totals here are conservative.

Country attribution is the weak link. The extraction gives locations per story, not per entity, so a victim's country is the one it co-occurs with most across its coverage — a US newspaper reporting on Moscow can come out tagged Russia. Countries from Wikidata P17 are the sound ones and are badged as such; the toggle above restricts the whole dashboard to them. Attacker countries have no such source, so the attacker map is coverage geography and the side coding is the better guide to who did it.
